## The short answer

A percentile is not a report card. It says where your child's weight or length sits compared with many other children of the same age and sex, using the WHO growth standards. The 50th is the middle of that group. It is not the goal, and it is not "normal" in the sense of correct. A child who stays quietly on their own line is growing, even when the number looks smaller or larger than you expected.

What usually matters more than the single number, when you look at the curve: is the direction steady, and does it fit what you see in ordinary days?

## What the curve can show

The WHO curves come from large groups. They describe a spread. They do not describe whether your evening was hard, whether milk is flowing freely right now, or whether the baby is in a stretch of looking longer and gaining very little. So you read the curve together with the child, not instead of the child.

A single weighing can mislead. A full diaper, a different time of day, a different scale at the clinic: one point jumps, and the fright is larger than the gap. Several points in a row tell the story. A line that follows its own path is usually reassuring, even when it does not pass through the middle.

### Your own line

Growing well often means staying near your own percentile. A jump across several bands, downward or upward, over more than one measurement, is a reason to look at it with your doctor. A single outlier is often the scale, not the child. Once you have heard that, the next appointment feels less like a trial.

### Weight and length together

Weight alone can tell the wrong film. A long child often sits higher on weight and is not "too heavy" because of it. Length and weight belong together, and the clinician sees both. If you only have one number in your head, bring the other. Otherwise you search one half of the story in the evening and receive a whole worry.

## What you can bring to a checkup

- the recent weights with dates, not only today's
- the lengths, if you have them
- a rough sense of the milk feeds and, if you have started, the solids
- your actual question, in one sentence, before the appointment talks it into pieces

You do not have to build a spreadsheet. A curve you can open is enough. The checkup stays the conversation. The numbers are the memory you cannot trust once you are under stress. "About the same as last time" is a sentence that gets smaller in the waiting room than it felt at home.

If a number unsettles you, say so. The curve is not a test you have to pass, and the doctor can place a point that, at home, you can only stare at.

## How Moonli shows weight and length

### WHO percentiles next to the day

Weight and length sit on the WHO curves, beside sleep and food. You see the line in the context of the day, not in a table made only of numbers. A leap, a stretch of small appetite, a growth in length: those sit closer together.

### No target the app assigns

Moonli does not tell you that you ought to land on the 50th. The curve shows where you are. Whether that fits your child stays with you and with the checkup. An app that marks one band as the goal turns a spread into a grade. We do not want that.

### A picture for the appointment

Before the checkup you have the course with you, instead of one number from memory. A PDF export for the clinic is not there yet. Until then, the view of the curve is what you can bring: the points, the dates, the line. That is already more than a photo of the kitchen scale.

## Before the next appointment

Weigh as often as it serves you, and not more often only because the curve would otherwise look "empty". Enter weight and length with the date. See whether the line is following its path.

If several points leave that path, that is a sentence for the doctor, not an evening with a search engine. If the line lies quietly and you still have an uneasy feeling, that is a sentence for the doctor too. The curve settles many questions. It does not have to be the last one.
