## The short answer

You know the moment. A minute ago the baby was with you, looking, feeding, a fist against a cheek. Ten minutes later everything is too much: the light, your voice, their own body. A wake window is the stretch in between. After waking, a baby can carry the world for a while. Then they are tired enough for the next sleep. Wait past that, and tiredness often tips into overwhelm. Putting them down gets harder, even though everyone in the room is exhausted.

The stretch grows over the months. The table below is a range drawn from what many families see. It is a frame you can lean on. It does not grade you.

## Wake windows by age

In the first year a baby does not yet have a reliable day-and-night rhythm. They get tired, sleep, wake, get tired again. How long the awake stretch holds depends on age, and just as much on last night, on a cold, on a leap where suddenly everything is new. On some days the row in the table is exactly right. On others it is only where looking begins.

| Age | Wake window, a guide |
| --- | --- |
| 0–6 weeks | 45–60 minutes |
| 2–3 months | 75–90 minutes |
| 4–6 months | 90–120 minutes |
| 7–9 months | 2–3 hours |
| 10–12 months | 3–4 hours |

Read every row as a span. A four-month-old can be happily awake for a hundred minutes on a bright morning and tip over after eighty on the day after a short night. Once you have seen that, the table gets kinder. It takes the guessing out of the evening, when nobody can quite remember when the last nap actually was.

### 0 to 6 weeks

Sleep still comes in pieces, and that is all right. A window can be shorter than an hour. What carries you is closeness, milk, and a quiet place. Planning a long nap down to the minute wears both of you out. If the eyes are closing while you are still thinking "it cannot be time yet", it may already be time.

### 2 to 3 months

This is where many parents breathe out, because something repeats. A clear awake stretch after a feed, then a yawn, then the fuss. The table turns into a day you recognise. It stays a tender day. One rough morning knocks it over, and tomorrow it is back.

### 4 to 6 months

Awake time gets easier to picture, which is exactly why overtiredness hurts so much. Bedtime often escalates because the window was already closed, not because a ritual was missing. A longer song rarely repairs that. An earlier try at putting them down sometimes does.

### 7 to 12 months

Teeth, first foods, the work of rolling, sitting, crawling: the day gets fuller, the span gets longer, and the outliers do too. A baby who is practising standing can be wide awake at half past four and completely done at half past five. The table holds the frame. Your eyes hold the single day.

## How you can tell the window has closed

Overtired babies often fall asleep worse. That feels unfair, and it is common. Typical signs are a sudden cry the moment you lie them down, naps of twenty or thirty minutes, sleep that only happens in motion, and an evening that turns wired and almost hunted even while the eyes are already rubbing.

The earlier signs are quieter, and they are easy to miss when you are tired too. A yawn. The gaze leaves. Movements get jerky. The game that was working a minute ago falls apart. If you write down for three to five days when that happens, you will see your pattern faster than with a clock alone.

The sleep space stays plain and safe beside all of this. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends, for the first year, back sleeping, a firm surface, and no loose bedding in the sleep space. A wake window does not change that. It only says when the moment to lie down is often kinder.

## One week of watching only

Log sleep for three to five days without rebuilding the whole day. Beside that, notice only when tiredness became visible: the yawn, the look away, the game that stops. At the end of the week, lay those moments next to the row for your baby's age.

If you are regularly well past it and bedtime is a fight every evening, try the next attempt earlier. If you are under it and the baby is content and awake, there is nothing to repair. The range is a frame you measure against your own days. A stranger's plan from the internet has not lived your nights.
