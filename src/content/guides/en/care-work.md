## The short answer

Care work is rarely only the diaper that is due right now. It is also knowing that it will be due in a minute, that the bottle has not been washed, that at three the last side was the left one, and that the checkup is tomorrow. That invisible part often stays with one person, even when both "help". It gets smaller when both of you see the same day, without one of you having to narrate it to the other.

The WHO describes the time after birth as one in which mental strain is real. A shared record is not therapy. It removes one layer of remembering. For many people that is exactly the layer still present in the evening, after the baby is already asleep.

## What usually stays with one person

The tasks can be split. The remembering, less so. Whoever knows when the last nap was, which side is next, whether the cream is running out, is carrying the day, even while the other person is holding the baby. Questions like "so, how was the night?" sound loving, and they still land on the person who has had the answer in their head all morning.

That is not a flaw in character. It is a tilt that appears because information sticks in one place: in memory, in a chat, on a note in the kitchen. Whoever cannot see the note cannot carry it. Whoever always has to write it is never really free.

It does not get fairer because one person explains more carefully. It gets fairer when the explanation is no longer needed. The record is there. Both of you can look. The person who has capacity takes the next task, without needing a briefing first.

## One record instead of a message

A message is already work. It has to be written, read, answered. "Left was at 2:40, bottle 90 ml at 5, nap 40 minutes." That is a protocol a tired person types so the other one is up to date. A shared record makes that message unnecessary. Whoever wakes up can see it. Whoever comes home from work can see it. Nobody has to pour the night into sentences one more time.

That does not mean you stop talking. It means the talking is yours again, for "how are you", and not for reconstructing the day. The difference is quiet, and it is very large.

## What fair does not mean

Fair does not mean every diaper is split exactly in half. Life is uneven: shifts, breastfeeding, who wakes more easily at night. Fair means the knowledge and the planning do not land automatically on one person, and that the invisible list eventually becomes visible to the other person too.

A conversation about the larger picture is still needed. Who takes the appointments, who takes the laundry, who keeps the vaccination in mind. An app does not hold that conversation. It can shorten the small, daily negotiation, so there is still strength left for the large one. If mood shifts for weeks, you do not recognise yourself, or nothing holds anymore, that is the path to a midwife, a doctor or a counselling service. Everyday relief and help from outside belong together when the ground gets thin.

## How Moonli makes the record shared

### Parent sync

Both parents see sleep, feeds and diapers in real time. What one person logs is there for the other, without being forwarded. The day stops being a private archive.

### Honour wheel, points, compliments

The honour wheel draws a concrete task when the negotiation gets sticky. Points and a compliment are a small recognition for work that otherwise stays invisible. That is play, not a wage system. It says: I saw that you did this. On some evenings that sentence is worth more than a perfectly fair tally.

### The record stays free to read

Tracking sleep, feeds, diapers and growth stays free. The shared view for two, the honour wheel and the points belong to Moonli Premium. The information about the day should not disappear behind a paywall, or one person carries the knowledge again and the other only carries the idea of an app.

## An evening when the knowledge does not stay with one person

Log what happens anyway: the feed, the sleep, the diaper. In the evening, do not look at who has more points. Look at whether the other person could know the day without asking you.

If the answer is yes, a piece of mental load has moved. If the answer is no, it is often not a lack of goodwill. The information still lives in one head. There it is hard to share. On the shared record it is only a glance.
