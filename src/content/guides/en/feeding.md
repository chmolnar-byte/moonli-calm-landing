## The short answer

At three in the morning nobody wants to fill in a form. You want to know which side was next, or how much of the bottle actually went, and you want to still know it tomorrow without reconstructing the night in your head. For breastfeeding, side and a rough length are usually enough. For a bottle, amount and time are enough. More than that makes the night heavier without making the morning any wiser.

The WHO and the NHS describe breastfeeding as the baby's need. Frequent feeds in the early weeks are normal. An app that turns that into a target makes the day tighter than it already is.

## What is worth remembering

It is only a few things, and those are exactly the ones that vanish when you are half asleep:

- which side, or whether it was both
- roughly how long it went
- for a bottle, the amount that was actually drunk, not the amount you poured
- the time, so the gap is visible in the morning

What rarely helps: logging every tiny pause, a note about the colour of a milk stain, a comparison with someone else's baby. That builds a protocol no decision comes out of. You lie there afterwards more awake than before.

### The night

At night, the tap has to be big enough for a hand that is already holding the baby. A button for left, right or bottle is what that is for. You do not have to type while they are feeding, and you do not have to politely complete a whole screen. One tap when it is over is enough. The rest of the night can stay dark.

### The next morning

The note becomes valuable when the second person sees the same record. Otherwise the whole night stays in the memory of the person who was awake. That is a quiet unfairness you mostly notice the third time you explain which side it was at half past two. A shared record takes that retelling away.

## Reading gaps without building a target

A gap of two or three hours is common in the first months. That can steady you at four in the morning, when you are thinking this cannot possibly be happening again. It is still no proof that everything is fine, and a shorter gap is no proof that something is wrong. Evenings often bring several short feeds in a row. People call that cluster feeding. It can feel as if you are doing nothing else. For many babies it is a phase, not a mistake in the plan.

Few wet diapers, a baby who barely wakes properly to feed, pain while latching: that is not a question for a chart. It belongs with a midwife, a feeding specialist or a doctor. The log can prepare the conversation, because you can say what the last days actually held, instead of estimating it out of exhaustion.

If you are breastfeeding and unsure whether "enough" is arriving, the honest answer is often this: at home you see that in diapers, weight, and the impression of someone who knows infants. You do not see it in a minute counter. The minutes help you switch sides and remember the day. They are not a report card.

## How this sits in Moonli

### Breast and bottle in one place

Side, length or amount sit next to sleep and diapers. You see the day as a sequence. First the feed, then the nap, then hunger again. Three scraps of paper on the counter tell the same story worse, the moment one of them is missing.

### Quick buttons for one hand

You decide what one tap records. If at night you almost only need the side, that is the button. The bar follows your night, not a template built for a different family.

### A reminder only if you want one

A nudge toward the next feed is possible. It stays off until you turn it on. Some people want to see the gap and hear nothing. Some are glad to be reminded, because otherwise the day blurs. Both are fine. Tracking stays free, even when the other parent sees the same record.

## A night that is still readable in the morning

Log only what you can do with one hand: side or amount, and the time that is already now. In the morning you see the gaps as a row. You recognise an evening cluster because several short entries sit close together, not because you feel bad about it.

If you would otherwise have to explain the morning to the person who slept, the entry is already there. The question at the breakfast table gets shorter. What remains is how you both are. Which side it was at 2:40 does not have to be lifted out of anyone's memory.
