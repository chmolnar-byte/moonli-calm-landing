## The short answer

There is a moment when nothing works anymore. Carried, fed, changed, and the crying goes on. The phone is the next thing the hand finds, because it delivers something at once: a song, a face, thirty seconds of quiet. The cost is that the screen becomes the soother, and that you often feel worse afterwards than you did before.

There are reaches that can hold the same moment without the display ending up in the child's hand. A steady sound. A short story that you hold. The phone put down, and the record of the day still there. None of them is magic. All of them are a way out you can decide in advance, while you are still clear. In the middle of crying, a good plan is rarely invented.

## Why the screen is so close

It is always there, it is bright, and it works. For a tired parent, "it works" is the only criterion in that minute. The American Academy of Pediatrics and the WHO advise keeping screen time for the youngest children very small. That is easy to read and hard to do when you have been walking up and down for an hour and your shoulder burns.

The difference that holds up in ordinary life is small and concrete. A screen the child holds and watches is different from a phone that, in your hand, plays a sound or a voice while you stay with the child. One becomes the habit of soothing. The other is a tool you can put down again. You are still allowed to hate the moment. A better reach does not make it romantic.

## Three reaches that are not a screen in a child's hands

### Steady sound

White noise, or a calm rush of sound, covers the door, the dishwasher, the other child. For some babies it is a background under which the body comes down. It is not a sleep medicine, and it does not belong in the bed as a loose device. The sound comes from the phone you are holding, or from one that lies safely aside. If it does not help, you are allowed to turn it off. You have not done anything wrong.

### A short story

A story has an ending. That is its kindness. It lasts a few minutes, the voice is calm, and the light does not have to fall on the child's face. You can use the same story on every difficult evening until it is as familiar as a song. Familiar voices, including a grandparent's when they are not there, are closeness. They are not a programme that has to fill the evening.

### The phone down, the record stays

Sometimes the right reach is to put the phone down and only be there. That is hard when the day also lives on the phone: the last feed, the last sleep. The phone-down prompt is the nudge to do exactly that, without losing the overview. The day stays saved. Your hands are with the child. If you still need the display afterwards, that is a person in a hard moment, not a broken principle.

## What to check first

Before a sound or a story is supposed to be the answer, it is worth a look at whether something simple is still open. Hunger, a wet diaper, a wake window that closed long ago, a bodysuit that is too warm. Soothing on top of an unmet need gets loud, however lovely the story is. If the breathing worries you, the crying feels different from usual, or the baby is barely feeding, that is not a moment for a sound. That is a moment for help.

The sleep place stays safe: on the back, a firm surface, nothing loose beside them. A speaker does not replace that rule. It sits beside it, not inside it.

## How Moonli puts these reaches side by side

Sounds and stories live in the same app as the day. You do not have to switch between a soothing app and a scrap of paper while the crying is already there. Quality time and the phone-down prompt remind you to let the phone go again. Recorded voices bring a person into the room who is not there just now.

### Shadow play, with a guide

Sometimes what helps is something your hands make, not the display. Moonli has shadow play with a short guide: which animal, which fingers, which step. You hold the phone, the light comes from the lamp, the picture appears on the wall. The child looks at the shadow, not at a glowing screen in their own hands. If you do not have the sequence in your head yet, you do not have to invent it while someone is crying. The guide is already there.

None of that is a promise that it will then be quiet. It is a short list you know before you need it. Tracking sleep and feeds stays free, even if you rarely use the sound.

## A reach you choose beforehand

On a quiet day, choose one reach. A sound, a story, a shadow play with the guide from the app, or putting the phone down. Say it to yourself concretely enough that you can still find it in the noise: "rabbit on the wall first, phone with me, two minutes of just staying."

If the moment comes and you still reach for the display, the evening is not lost. Tomorrow the reach is there again. A child learns soothing over many such evenings, and so do you. Nobody passes the first try only because they decided something beforehand.
