## The short answer

For many families the 4-month sleep regression is the first phase in which baby sleep becomes “different”: more waking, shorter naps, harder to put down – even though the last few weeks had seemed easier. Behind it there is often no mistake in your ritual, but sleep that is becoming more grown-up: lighter and deeper parts, more transitions at which your baby notices that they are lying alone in the cot.

You do not need new training on the first restless evening. What holds: a safe sleep space, a small [evening routine](/en/guides/baby-sleep-routine/), and a look at the [wake window](/en/guides/baby-wake-windows/) before you add more steps. The American Academy of Pediatrics keeps to back sleeping, a firm surface and no loose bedding. Restlessness does not change these rules.

## Why around the fourth month?

In the first months sleep often looks like one block: in, out, milk, back in. Around the fourth month the sleep cycle becomes more structured. Many babies wake more easily between cycles and then need help falling asleep again – help that deep newborn sleep used to “deliver” on its own.

At the same time the day becomes more alert. Reaching, focusing, first movements: the brain is practising. At night the same thing can keep running. That is why the phase feels so personal. It looks as if your baby has forgotten the routine. Often they mainly have more awareness of the moment of being alone in the cot.

The NHS describes simple supports: a dark, quiet room, a familiar order of steps, patience. That does not replace a diagnosis. It keeps the frame small when, at three in the morning, you would most like to invent a new system.

## Typical signs

- more frequent waking at night, even if there had already been longer stretches
- daytime naps that end after 20–40 minutes
- fussing or crying when you lay your baby down, although the sequence is the same
- a baby who seems “tired and wired” at the same time
- new movements that are practised on through the night

Not every sign has to arrive at once. And not every restless week around month four is “the” regression: an infection, growth or an overly full day can feel the same. If your baby is hardly feeding, has a fever or you are worried about their breathing, the right step is your midwife or paediatrician – not a longer checklist.

## What helps in these weeks

### 1. Check the wake window earlier

Many evenings escalate because the window had already closed before the ritual started. Look at the last sleep, not only at the clock. The guide to [wake windows by age](/en/guides/baby-wake-windows/) helps you tell overtiredness apart from “regression”.

### 2. Shorten the ritual, do not lengthen it

Three familiar steps carry more than a new ten-part programme. Dim the lights, breastfeed or bottle, lay down. The song can stay if it carries you. If nothing works any more: a smaller circle, not a bigger one.

### 3. Keep the safe sleep space

Back sleeping, a firm surface, no loose bedding. Even if you comfort a lot at night: the sleep space stays the safe place. Questions about bed-sharing belong with advice on the ground, not in a blanket app recommendation.

### 4. Protect rest windows during the day

An afternoon with too much stimulation and too little sleep makes the night louder. That is not a reproach. It is the most common lever you can still influence the next day.

### 5. Do not turn one bad night into the new standard

The next evening you return to the edge of your routine. Without renegotiating the night before. Phases often fade when the signals stay the same and the day gets its edges back.

## Duration: what you can realistically expect

Many families expect about two to six weeks of more restless nights. Some babies need longer until the new cycles feel familiar. Afterwards sleep often stays lighter and more interrupted than in the newborn period – that is part of maturing, not automatically a lasting problem.

If after several weeks you are also worried about weight, feeding or your baby's general wellbeing, get a medical assessment. An app can track and put things in context. It cannot examine.

## What you can leave out this week

Skip the new sleep training on night one. Skip the comparison with the neighbours' child who is “already sleeping through”. Keep three signals, the safe space and a look at the window.
